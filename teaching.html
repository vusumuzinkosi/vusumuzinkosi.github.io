How I teach

  1. Look at the data early

    I track learner performance through the term so I can see who is falling behind while there is still time to help, not after the exam.

  2. Assess fairly and consistently

    I developed standardised assessment rubrics that were adopted across the department, so a mark means the same thing whoever does the marking.

  3. Explain it in plain language

    Most of my students do not love mathematics. Explaining it clearly to them every day is the same skill I now use to present model results to managers.

  4. Meet learners where they are

    At Mdumiseni High School I taught diverse learner groups and adapted materials to different learning needs. That habit stayed with me.

Where teaching meets data

Spotting at-risk students is a prediction problem. Deciding which learners get extra support with limited time is the same capacity question as sizing a fraud review queue or a churn call list. Moving into data science gave me better tools for questions I was already asking in the classroom.

I'm interested in education analytics, TVET research and training roles where both sides of that experience are useful. Let's talk.
